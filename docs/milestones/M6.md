`web_ws`'s 21 tests (oracles: the RFCs, zlib, and lib/std's UTF-8 check):
- SHA-1 (RFC 3174 §7.3's four vectors), base64 (RFC 4648 §10, and refusals), the accept key (RFC 6455 §1.3);
- UTF-8 (RFC 3629 §4: boundaries, overlongs, surrogates, above U+10FFFF, truncation, fail-fast positions);
- RFC 6455 §5.7's example frames, written and read, every reader input at every split;
- 22 frame violations, each with its close code;
- upgrade requests (§1.2's request; origins; version 8; missing fields) and subprotocol choice;
- the session over a scripted client:
  - the 101 and an echo, with a ping between fragments, at 1-, 3- and 1000-byte reads;
  - refusals: 400, 403, 426;
  - violations closed with 1002, 1007 and 1009;
  - keep-alive (a ping after 100 ms, 1011 at 200 ms) and a peer leaving (1006);
  - messages queued from another task;
  - fragments, and a server-initiated close with and without the peer's answer;
- inflate: RFC 7692 §7.2.3's five examples; zlib's dynamic block, a 9-bit window and context takeover; the limit;
  every truncation and a bit flip every 7 bits (never a panic, never past the limit);
- deflate round trips (empty, text, a 9-bit window, 100000 equal bytes, 70000 random bytes);
- negotiation: 15 offers against RFC 7692 §7.1; compressed frames (RSV1 rules, bad data 1007, a bomb 1009);
- a compressed echo through the session.

`ws.py`'s checks, against `examples/ws_echo`:
- **With `websockets`:**
  - permessage-deflate agreed, so zlib inflates every echo we compress, and an echo without it;
  - echoes of text (empty, Greek, 70000 characters, 88000 compressible) and 1 MiB of binary;
  - a ping answered, and a close code echoed (4000);
  - ten clients interleaved;
  - the same origin accepted, another refused with 403, an allowed one (`CW_ORIGIN`) accepted.
- **With raw sockets:**
  - version 8 gets 426 with `Sec-WebSocket-Version: 13`;
  - six malformed frames, each closed with its code;
  - 200 clients sending random bytes, after which an echo still works;
  - a slow consumer: another client is served meanwhile, and the stuck connection closes after the write deadline;
  - keep-alive: a ping after 200 ms, and 1011 when it goes unanswered.

The fuzzer's invariants:
1. The reader yields the same events whole or in random pieces, in both roles, with and without deflate.
2. Deflate then inflate gives back the input, over random windows of 8-15 bits.
3. Damaged compressed data never panics, never passes the limit, and inflates the same way twice.
4. The UTF-8 validator in pieces agrees with `StringView.fromUtf8`.

## Notes

- **Compression is slow.** Inflate decodes a bit at a time (as zlib's `puff.c` does), and both directions grow
  vectors a byte at a time. Autobahn's 12.2.15 (1000 messages of 65 KiB) took 17 s. Table-driven decoding and bulk
  copies are the obvious next steps; they belong with M9's benchmarks.
